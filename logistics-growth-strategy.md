# QED / Nomadiq / QSHIP: scaling a logistics model

**Context:** A Nigerian road-freight operation began as QE Distributions (QED) in March 2016. A subsequent Nomadiq plan explored an asset-light logistics positioning; the 2018 QSHIP business plan described a broader B2B freight strategy. These are related planning materials and phases of the venture, **not three independent success stories**.

**My role:** Co-founder and strategy/operations contributor in the QED lineage. This retrospective focuses on the reasoning in the historical plans; company-reported operating figures are attributed to those plans.

## The decision

An early national line-haul contract generated demand across regions. Owning enough vehicles and staffing offices everywhere would tie up capital, while relying on unrelated contractors without operating standards could weaken reliability. The business had to decide how to expand capacity and coverage, manage service quality, and reduce dependence on one anchor client.

## Evidence used

The Nomadiq plan describes more than **3,000 trips in the first 11 months**. Both the Nomadiq and QSHIP plans state that the operation reached **35 of Nigeria's 36 states** and handled **98% of Konga's interstate line haulage** at the time described. These are historical assertions in company planning documents, not independently audited figures.

The plans say the strategy drew on leadership discussions, stakeholder interviews, desk research, historical operating data, and market analysis. The 2018 QSHIP plan records a useful sequence:

- **March 2016:** QED acquired seven trucks for initial routes.
- **May 2016:** After additional routes, it began using underutilized third-party freight assets.
- **June–October 2016:** The team pursued clients beyond Konga and onboarded regional franchise partners to help replace a network of directly managed offices.
- **2017:** The plan says the business narrowed its focus toward B2B ecommerce and FMCG customers and reviewed costs and operational efficiency.

## Strategic choice and tradeoffs

The proposed model combined a small amount of controlled capacity with a network of truck owners and regional partners. This offered geographic reach without owning every vehicle, but it increased the need for partner selection, route visibility, service standards, and exception handling. The QSHIP plan identifies **processes, technology, and service quality** as the main sources of durable advantage; technology is described as an enabler of visibility, reporting, risk management, and planning.

The decision rule was therefore more demanding than “add trucks to meet demand.” For a new route or customer, the team needed to weigh expected contribution after partner and operating costs, reliable capacity, service-level risk, working-capital needs, and exposure to a single customer or partner. A forecast based on market size alone could not answer those questions.

## Results versus plans

The planning documents report completed route expansion and the shift toward third-party capacity. The QSHIP plan also reports that monthly revenue first exceeded **₦24 million** during the November/December 2016 surge and **₦35 million** in a nonsurge month in January/February 2017. Those figures are document-reported milestones and would need reconciliation against underlying accounts before being used as independently verified performance claims.

By contrast, QSHIP's proposed **$2 million** fundraising round, **2018–2022** revenue projections, and target of at least **100% annual growth** are plans and forecasts. This case does not present them as achieved outcomes.

## What this demonstrates

The case illustrates a consulting approach: start with an operating bottleneck, trace the available evidence, compare capacity models, make the tradeoffs explicit, and distinguish actual milestones from projections. That evidence discipline also matters in AI response evaluation and fact-checking, especially when a fluent answer might merge company claims, forecasts, and verified results.

*Source basis: “Nomadiq Business Plan Feb 2017”; “QSHIP 2018–2022 Business Plan v07022018.” The QSHIP plan also supplies the dated QED history. The original plans are not reproduced here.*
